Kitchen Water Damage Help in Temecula

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. For homeowners in Temecula, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Signs to Look For

  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Laminate or wood floor that is lifting or cupping where you stand at the dishwasher
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

Drips at the Sink Drain

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

Toe Kick Space

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

Layers of Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Water gets trapped between the layers, where it cannot evaporate and will not show up on the surface.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first hour

Water Runs Under

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Day 3 to 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

What We Do

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Stop the Source

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.
