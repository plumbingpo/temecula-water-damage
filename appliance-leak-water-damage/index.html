Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Temecula

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

For homeowners in Temecula, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. In laundry rooms it means the wall behind the machine and the rooms next door, or the ceiling below if the laundry is upstairs. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. Otherwise, use the breaker to cut its power.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Mop Up What You CanGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Our Process

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

1

Pull and Inspect

We disconnect and move the appliance, confirm the supply is off, and trace the water path under cabinets, beneath flooring and into nearby walls with moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Carpet and floors are cleared of standing water with extraction equipment. Where water is trapped under cabinets or floating floors, we remove toe kicks or lift boards to reach it.

Dehumidifiers and air movers are set up, and when it is possible, directed airflow dries hardwood and cabinets where they sit so nothing has to be torn out.

We apply antimicrobial treatment wherever discharge from the washer or the dishwasher made contact. Before the appliance is put back, a last set of readings confirms the floor under it is no longer damp.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Dishwasher parts that wear out, including the door gasket, the drain hose and the seal on the pump.
  • A kinked or cracked ice maker line, or one whose fitting has worked loose.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Rooms Next Door

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Cabinet Damage

Toe kicks and cabinet sides take on water from underneath when a dishwasher leaks. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. The drywall stains and sags, and the insulation above it stays wet.
