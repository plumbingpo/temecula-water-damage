Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Help in Temecula

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

This water is not clean. It has come through soil and often carries silt, bacteria and whatever was in the drain tile, so we treat it as Category 2 at best and Category 3 if it has sat or mixed with sewage. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Keep your hands out of the water.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Do not wait for the water to drain away by itself.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Standard homeowners coverage generally leaves out sump overflow and backup, while flood coverage is bought separately and follows other rules. Often the only coverage comes from an added endorsement for water backup and sump overflow, which has limits of its own. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Pump Out the Basement

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Wash and Disinfect

An antimicrobial product goes on after the concrete, the studs and any kept belongings have been washed. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Verify

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • The pump being unplugged by accident or sitting on an outlet that tripped.
  • The float getting caught on the side of the basin or tangled in its cord.
  • More water flowing into the pit than an undersized pump can move.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Wall Framing

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Stored Belongings

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. Water reaching burners, controls or wiring can ruin them, and each appliance must be inspected before it is used again.

Outlets and Wiring

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.
