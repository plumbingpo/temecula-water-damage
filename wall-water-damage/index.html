Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Temecula

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

What It Looks Like

  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • A tide line or discoloration running along the bottom of the wall
  • Trim along the floor that has puffed up, twisted or come loose
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

Insulation Holding Water

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Every wall involved is tested on its two faces, and so is the adjacent flooring.

Walls You Cannot See

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.

Our Process

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Open Up or Air Out

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Wicking Begins

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.
