Attic Water Damage: What Temecula Homeowners Should Know

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. In Temecula, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • Bubbling or peeling ceiling paint in a room on the top floor
  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
  • Water dripping from, or staining around, a ceiling light or bathroom fan
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • A patch of insulation that is flatter, more matted, or darker than the rest
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Every day, a fan duct that stops short of the roof releases humid bathroom air into the attic. That moisture condenses on the cold sheathing and looks just like a roof leak.

Mold on Sheathing

With poor ventilation, mold commonly grows on the bottom of the roof deck. A wide area may be covered by the time somebody climbs up to check.

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.

Insulation That Stops Working

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that makes it through the roof falls into the insulation and is absorbed. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

After months

Rot Sets In

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.
