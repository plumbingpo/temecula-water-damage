Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Temecula

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. Until someone closes the cold water valve, it keeps running. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

Our Temecula team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Do This First

Your First Steps

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.

What Happens After You Call

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • Rust at the connections above the tank, either the flex lines or the inlet and outlet fittings.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Adjacent Room Flooring

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

Items Stored Near the Heater

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Garage Wall Base

Water crossing a garage slab soaks the bottom plate and drywall of the wall shared with the house, then moves into the flooring on the living side.

Closet Walls

When the heater closet is indoors, its drywall draws water upward and passes it to rooms on the far side. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Whether the platform or subfloor under the tank has to be rebuilt before a new heater goes in.
  • The length of time the tank went on refilling until someone closed the supply valve.
  • The location of the heater, since a garage leak costs less than one in an interior closet or attic.
  • Amount of contents affected, since wet belongings have to be relocated, dried or discarded.
